Daisy Chain Fields: The Ultimate Unity Ticket

Aug 29, 20255 min read

The successful debut of the Daisy Chain Fields festival this year highlights the importance of targeting your audience based on similar needs, interests, and beliefs.

Music marketers, are you aware of the power segmentation holds in marketing?

Market segmentation is a process that divides a broad market into smaller groups of consumers with shared characteristics. Thus, instead of using a one-size-fits-all when creating marketing campaigns or brainstorming strategies, segmentation is used to tailor the advertising efforts to each distinct group.

Inspired by Sarah McLachlan's festival from the late '90s, Lilith Fair, Olivia Rodrigo founded Daisy Chain Fields, a charity-driven festival featuring an all-women lineup and multiple pop-ups following themes of creative expression. Flower crowns, bracelet making, wellness zones, immersive art, and good music — all in support of women's rights.

Let The Segmentation Times Roll!

Marketers — it's important to understand that different audiences call for different approaches.

Successful marketing segmentation will often fall into one or more of these categories. Psychographic focuses on why consumers buy (based on beliefs and lifestyle), while demographic highlights who they are (age, gender, income), geographical pinpoints where they are, and lastly, behavioral identifies how they interact (purchase patterns).

But how is Daisy Chain successful?

With the help of psychographic segmentation, they targeted an audience with shared values and beliefs in women's empowerment and bodily autonomy and a strong desire for community building and bonding in a safe place.

In addition to selling out tickets in a 30 minute time span, the festival raised well over $20 million for female-centered charities around the world.

The question from consumers now? “How far in advance can we RSVP for next year?”

Remember, if done right, segmentation will book you a one-way ticket to celebration!
